---

title: How do I know if my plant is thirsty?
category: watering
---

Watering on a fixed schedule is the surest way to overwater. A plant's need for water changes with the season, the light, the warmth of the room and the size of the pot. Before reaching for the watering can, ask the plant… and its soil.

## The finger test

Push a finger 2 to 3 cm into the soil. If it's dry at that depth, it's time to water. If it still feels cool or damp, wait a few days. For cacti and succulents, wait until the soil is dry all the way down.

## The weight of the pot

Lift the pot right after watering to remember how heavy it is. A thirsty plant is noticeably lighter. With a little practice, it's the quickest test of all.

## Signs on the leaves

- **Soft or drooping leaves**, dry soil: the plant is thirsty.
- **Soft leaves**, soggy soil: the roots lack air, it's too much water.
- **Yellow leaves at the bottom of the plant**: often watering too often.
- **Dry brown tips**: air too dry or irregular watering.

## Watering well

When it's time, water generously, until water runs out of the bottom of the pot. Empty the saucer 15 minutes later: standing water rots the roots. A thorough watering now and then beats a few drops every day.
