---

title: Rooting cuttings in water
category: propagation
---

Rooting in water is the simplest way to propagate your plants, and you get to watch the roots grow. Pothos, philodendrons, monsteras, tradescantias and spider plants all take to it very well.

## Taking the cutting

1. Pick a healthy stem, ideally in spring or summer.
2. Cut it just below a **node**, the small bump where leaves and roots grow from. For a monstera, the node needs an aerial root, or the cutting won't grow.
3. Keep one or two leaves at the top, and remove any that would sit in the water.

## Into the water

- Dip the node in a glass of room-temperature water, with the leaves out of the water.
- Put the glass in a bright spot, but not in full sun.
- **Change the water every week**, so it stays clear and oxygenated.

The first roots show up within two to six weeks, depending on the plant and the season.

## Moving to soil

Once the roots are 3 to 5 cm long, plant the cutting in a small pot of light potting mix. Keep the soil a little moister than usual for the first two weeks: roots grown in water need to get used to soil.

Some plants, such as pothos, can also live in water for a long time, with a little fertilizer now and then.
