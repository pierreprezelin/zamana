---

title: Repotting without stressing your plant
category: basics
---

A houseplant needs a new pot roughly every two to three years, when it starts running out of room.

## Signs it's time

- Roots poke out of the drainage hole or circle around the inside of the pot.
- Water runs straight through the pot: there are more roots than soil.
- The plant grows less, despite good watering and light.

## The right time

In spring, when growth picks up again. The plant heals quickly and makes the most of its new space right away. Avoid repotting a plant in bloom, or one resting in winter.

## Step by step

1. **Water the day before**: the root ball will slide out more easily.
2. **Unpot** by turning the pot upside down, your hand flat on the soil.
3. **Untangle** roots that circle around, and trim any that are black or mushy.
4. **Set** the plant in a pot 2 to 4 cm wider, at the same depth as before.
5. **Fill in** with fresh potting mix, pressing lightly.
6. **Water** once, then leave the plant alone, without fertilizer, for a month.

A plant may look tired for a few days after repotting: that's normal, it's settling in.
