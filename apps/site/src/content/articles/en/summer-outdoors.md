---

title: Taking your plants outside in summer
category: seasons
---

Many houseplants love spending the summer on a balcony or in a garden. They gain light, air and vigour, as long as you get them used to it.

## The right time

Wait until nights no longer drop below 12 to 15 °C, usually in late spring. Zamana can warn you about cold or heat for your outdoor plants.

## Getting them used to it

The sun outside is much stronger than behind glass. A plant suddenly put in full sun burns within hours.

1. **The first week**, place it in full shade.
2. **The second week**, give it the softer morning sun.
3. **After that**, only the plants that love it, such as cacti or citrus, can move to full sun.

Most tropical foliage plants are better off in partial shade all summer.

## Outside, everything changes

- **Water more often**: wind and heat dry the soil quickly.
- **Watch out for storms and strong wind**, which break stems and knock pots over.
- **Check the leaves** regularly: aphids and spider mites are more common outdoors.

## Coming back in

Bring the plants in before nights get cold, checking under the leaves and the soil, so you don't bring pests into the house.
