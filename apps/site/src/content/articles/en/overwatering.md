---

title: Saving an overwatered plant
category: watering
---

Too much water kills far more houseplants than drought. Soaked roots run out of air, suffocate and then rot.

## The signs

- Soft, yellow leaves, starting at the bottom of the plant.
- Soil that stays wet several days after watering, sometimes with a musty smell.
- A stem turning black or soft at the base.
- Small black flies buzzing around the pot.

## What to do

1. **Stop watering** and empty the saucer.
2. **Take the plant out of its pot** to look at the roots. Healthy roots are firm and light. Rotten ones are brown, mushy and come apart.
3. **Cut off the damaged roots** with clean scissors.
4. **Repot** in fresh, dry potting mix, in a pot with drainage holes, without reusing the old mix if it held rot.
5. **Wait** a week before the next watering, then resume sparingly.

If almost all the roots have rotted, take a healthy cutting: the mother plant is often lost, but its offspring can start again.

## To keep it from happening again

Only water when the top of the soil is dry, use a pot with drainage holes and always empty the saucer.
