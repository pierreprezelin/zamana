---

title: Direct, indirect or low light?
category: light
---

Plant labels talk about "bright indirect light" or "partial shade", but what does that mean in your home? Our eyes adapt very well and fool us: a room that looks bright to us can be quite dark for a plant.

## Direct light

The sun's rays reach the plant for several hours: the sill of a south- or west-facing window. That's the realm of cacti, succulents, citrus and Mediterranean plants. Other plants risk burns, especially behind glass in midsummer.

## Indirect light

A very bright room, but with no sun on the leaves: one or two metres from a south-facing window, behind a sheer curtain, or near an east-facing window. Most houseplants prefer this, like monstera, ficus or pothos.

## Low light

At the back of a room, near a north-facing window or in a hallway. Few plants truly thrive there, but some cope: ZZ plant, snake plant, cast-iron plant or pothos.

## The shadow test

At noon, hold your hand 30 cm above the spot:

- **sharp, crisp shadow**: direct light;
- **blurry shadow**: indirect light;
- **barely visible shadow**: low light.

A plant that stretches, turns pale or leans towards the window lacks light. Dry brown patches on exposed leaves, on the other hand, are a sign of sunburn.

_In the northern hemisphere. In the southern hemisphere, swap south and north._
