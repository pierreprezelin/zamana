---

title: Spotting and treating mealybugs and scale
category: pests
---

Mealybugs and scale insects are among the most common houseplant pests. Spotted early, they're easy to deal with.

## Spotting them

- **Mealybugs**: small white, cottony clumps where leaves meet stems, along the stems or under the leaves.
- **Scale insects**: small brown bumps, round or oval, stuck to stems and leaf veins.

Either way, the leaves often turn sticky: that's honeydew, a sugary liquid they give off. It sometimes attracts sooty mould, a black fungus.

## Treating them

1. **Isolate the plant** to protect its neighbours.
2. **Remove them by hand** with a cotton bud dipped in 70% rubbing alcohol: it dissolves their waxy coating.
3. **Shower the plant** with lukewarm water, covering the soil.
4. **Spray** a soft soap solution (one tablespoon per litre of lukewarm water) over the whole plant, including under the leaves.
5. **Repeat** every week for a month: eggs hatch in waves.

## Preventing them

Check every new plant before placing it near the others, as well as plants brought in from the garden in autumn. Very dry air helps them settle in: a little humidity and a regular look under the leaves are often enough.
