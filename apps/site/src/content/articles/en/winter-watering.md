---

title: Watering in winter
category: seasons
---

In winter, days get shorter and most houseplants slow down, or even stop growing. They drink much less, and it's the season when the most plants get drowned.

## Water less often

Keep the finger test habit: only water when the soil is dry 2 to 3 cm down. Depending on the plant and the room, the interval can double compared with summer. Zamana also adapts the watering pace to the rest season if you have chosen your climate.

## Special cases

- **Cacti and succulents**: almost no water in the depth of winter, a small sip a month is enough. This dry rest helps them bloom.
- **Plants near a radiator**: warm air dries the soil faster. Check them more often, but don't water out of habit.
- **Plants blooming in winter** (orchids, cyclamens, Christmas cacti): they keep drinking regularly while in bloom.

## Good habits

- Water in the morning, with room-temperature water.
- Empty the saucer: cold, soggy soil is the roots' worst enemy.
- No fertilizer until spring comes back.

New leaves appearing in spring signal the restart: it's time to gradually return to the summer pace.

_In the southern hemisphere, winter runs from June to August._
