---

title: Getting rid of spider mites
category: health
---

Spider mites are tiny mites, barely visible to the naked eye. They love warm, dry air, and often thrive in winter near radiators.

## Spotting them

- Fine, silky webs between leaves and stems.
- Leaves speckled with tiny yellow or greyish dots, which end up drying out.
- Tiny moving dots under the leaves, visible when you tap a leaf over a sheet of white paper.

## Treating them

1. **Isolate the plant**: spider mites easily move from one plant to another.
2. **Shower it** with lukewarm water, especially under the leaves, to wash off as many mites and webs as possible.
3. **Wipe the leaves** one by one with a damp cloth and a drop of soft soap.
4. **Raise the humidity** around the plant: they hate it.
5. **Repeat** every four to five days for three weeks, to get rid of the new generations.

On a heavily infested plant, cut off the most damaged leaves.

## Preventing them

Keep the air from getting too dry, move plants away from radiators, and mist the most prone species now and then, such as calatheas, palms or crotons.
