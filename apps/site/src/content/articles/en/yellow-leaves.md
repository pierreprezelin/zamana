---

title: Why does my plant have yellow leaves?
category: health
---

A yellow leaf isn't necessarily serious. It all depends on where it appears and how many there are.

## One or two lower leaves

That's normal ageing: the plant lets go of its oldest leaves to feed the new ones. Remove them once they come off easily, without pulling.

## Several soft lower leaves

It's most often **too much water**. Check the soil: if it's still wet several days after watering, let it dry out and empty the saucer. If the stem goes soft at the base, look at the roots.

## Yellow, dry leaves and dry soil

The plant is **short of water**, or the air is very dry. Water thoroughly, then go back to a regular pace.

## Young leaves turning yellow

Pale new leaves, with veins that stay green, are often a sign of **a deficiency**, iron in particular. It's common with very hard water. A balanced fertilizer, or rainwater, helps the plant recover.

## The whole plant turns pale

It's **short of light**, especially if it's stretching at the same time. Move it closer to a window, gradually.

A yellowed leaf won't turn green again: what matters is that new ones grow healthy.
