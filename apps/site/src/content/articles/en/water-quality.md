---

title: Which water for your plants?
category: watering
---

Tap water suits the vast majority of houseplants. A few simple habits make it even gentler on them.

## Tap water

Let it stand for a few hours, or overnight, in the watering can: it reaches room temperature and some of the chlorine evaporates. Icy water can shock the roots of tropical plants.

## Limescale

Where water is hard, limescale leaves white marks on the soil and the pot, and can harm the most sensitive plants in the long run. If you see these deposits:

- scrape off the white crust on top of the soil;
- flush the pot with plenty of water now and then, to wash out the salts;
- use a filter jug for delicate plants.

## Rainwater

It's the ideal water: soft, with no limescale or chlorine. Collect it in a clean container and use it at room temperature. Orchids, carnivorous plants and azaleas especially like it. Carnivorous plants actually only tolerate rainwater or distilled water.

## To avoid

Water from a home water softener contains salt, which is bad for roots. Cooking water, even cooled, does nothing good either.
