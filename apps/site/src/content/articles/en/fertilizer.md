---

title: "Fertilizer: when and how much?"
category: basics
---

In a pot, the soil runs out of nutrients within a few months. Fertilizer gives the plant what its roots can no longer find, but too much does more harm than too little.

## When to feed

During the growing season, from spring to late summer, when the plant is putting out new leaves. In autumn and winter, most plants rest: stop the fertilizer.

Don't feed a plant that has just been repotted, that is sick or that is short of water either.

## How much

Follow the dose on the packaging, or halve it to be safe. Feeding every two to four weeks is enough for most foliage plants. Cacti and succulents only need two or three feeds a year.

Always pour fertilizer onto soil that is already moist: on dry soil, it can burn the roots.

## Which fertilizer

- **Foliage plants**: a fertilizer rich in nitrogen, for the leaves.
- **Flowering plants**: a fertilizer rich in potassium, for the blooms.
- **Cacti, orchids**: a dedicated, gentler fertilizer.

A white crust on the soil or burnt leaf tips are signs of too much: flush the pot with plenty of water and feed less often.
