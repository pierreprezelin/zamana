---

title: Spotting and treating thrips
category: health
---

Thrips are tiny, slender insects, black or yellowish, one to two millimetres long. Discreet and quick, they're often spotted late, by the damage they leave.

## Spotting them

- Silvery or pearly patches on the leaves, where they've emptied the cells.
- Small black dots on these patches: their droppings.
- Distorted or dried-out leaves and flowers.
- Thin little insects that jump or fly off when you shake the leaf.

## Treating them

1. **Isolate the plant** right away: thrips move quickly from one plant to another.
2. **Shower it** thoroughly, on top of and under the leaves.
3. **Spray diluted soft soap** over the whole plant.
4. **Put blue sticky traps** around, a colour that particularly attracts them.
5. **Repeat** every week for six weeks: their larvae hide in the soil, out of reach of treatments.

Replacing the top layer of soil helps get rid of the larvae.

## Preventing them

Like spider mites, thrips like warm, dry air. Keep some humidity around your plants, and always check a new plant before placing it near the others.
