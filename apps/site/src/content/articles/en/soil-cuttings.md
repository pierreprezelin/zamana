---

title: Rooting cuttings straight in soil
category: propagation
---

Many plants can be rooted straight in potting mix, without going through water. The roots you get are sturdier, and the cutting doesn't need to adapt afterwards.

## Succulents and cacti

1. Take a whole leaf (echeveria, jade plant) or a stem (aloe, prickly pear).
2. **Let it dry** for a few days, in the shade, until the cut forms a small callus: it won't rot.
3. Lay it on, or push it slightly into, cactus mix that is barely moist.
4. Only water once the first roots show, after two to four weeks.

## Foliage plants

For pileas, ficus, begonias or herbs:

1. Cut an 8 to 15 cm stem below a node, and remove the lower leaves.
2. Plant it in a small pot of light potting mix, with a little sand or perlite.
3. Water, then cover the pot with a clear plastic bag, to keep the air humid.
4. Put it in bright light without direct sun, and let some air in now and then.

Rooting hormone powder helps woody plants such as ficus, but it isn't essential.

## How to tell it worked

Some resistance when you tug very gently on the stem, or new leaves: the roots are there. Remove the bag and treat the cutting like a young plant.
