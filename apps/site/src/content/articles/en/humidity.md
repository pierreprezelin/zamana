---

title: Raising the humidity
category: basics
---

Many houseplants come from tropical forests, where the air is very humid. In our homes, especially in winter with the heating on, humidity often drops below 40%.

## Signs the air is too dry

- Dry brown leaf tips.
- Leaves curling up or yellowing at the edges.
- Flower buds dropping before they open.
- Spider mites moving in: they love dry air.

## Solutions that work

- **Group plants together**: together, they create a small, more humid microclimate.
- **A tray of clay pebbles** filled with water under the pot, without the bottom of the pot touching the water.
- **A humidifier** in the room, the most effective option in winter.
- **The bathroom**, if it's bright, for ferns and calatheas.

## What about misting?

Misting the leaves feels nice, but the effect only lasts a few minutes. It's mostly useful to clean the leaves and bother spider mites. Avoid it on plants with fuzzy leaves, such as African violets, which may rot.

Finally, keep your plants away from radiators and heating vents: the warm air they blow is the driest in the house.
